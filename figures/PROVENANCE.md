# Figure provenance

The figures in eglantine-marginalia are cut from engravings in works published between 1753 and 1792, with two exceptions: the houlette comes from Sébastien Leclerc’s device for the king’s tapestries of 1670, and the ox from an etching by Jacob Elias van Varelen that the Rijksmuseum dates only to between 1772 and 1840. All are long in the public domain. Thresholding converts the scans into ink masks with transparent backgrounds, and cleaning removes scenery, ground and neighbouring figures.

Faithful reproductions of public-domain flat artworks attract no new copyright in the United States under Bridgeman Art Library v. Corel or in the European Union under Article 14 of Directive 2019/790. The rights column distinguishes the original’s public-domain status from the scan terms stated by the hosting institution. A public-domain declaration for a work is not treated as a CC0 declaration for its scan.

## Mask preparation

A thresholding tool makes the masks from the original scans. Cropping and erasure remove paper, captions, neighbouring figures and scenery. Rotation or reflection sets the orientation where needed. The shipped transparent PNGs are reduced to 240 px on the long side.
